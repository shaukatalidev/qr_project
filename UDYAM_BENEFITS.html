What the Udyam certificate is actually worth to Qravio

Nearly every "40 benefits of Udyam registration" list online is written for a factory owner. Strip out the electricity tariffs, the machinery subsidies and the outright myths, and what remains for a six-month-old B2B SaaS in Bengaluru is five things that carry real money — one of which you should act on before you send your next invoice.

Tier 1

Worth money now

These three are live the moment the certificate exists, cost nothing or nearly nothing to claim, and are the reason the registration was worth doing.

Governing lawMSMED Act 2006, §15–16
Income Tax Act, §43B(h)
Companies Act, Form MSME-1

Cash flow · immediate

Your customers are now legally obliged to pay you within 45 days

This is the single biggest thing the certificate buys a B2B SaaS. Any buyer of Qravio's services must pay within 45 days of accepting the service where there is a written agreement, and within 15 days where there isn't. It has three sets of teeth, and the tax one is what actually changes behaviour:

  • Tax. Under §43B(h), in force since AY 2024–25, a buyer who misses the window cannot deduct that expense in the year it was incurred. The deduction slips to the year they actually pay. For a company with a March year-end, an unpaid Qravio invoice becomes a real addition to taxable profit.
  • Interest. The MSMED Act entitles you to compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate — punitive by design, and not waivable by contract.
  • Disclosure. Corporate buyers must file MSME-1 with the MCA every half-year (30 Apr / 31 Oct) naming every micro/small supplier left unpaid past 45 days. Non-filing costs them ₹20,000 plus ₹1,000 a day. Their finance teams already run MSME vendor ageing reports to avoid it.
The limit that catches people out

In Silpi Industries v. KSRTC (Supreme Court, 2021) it was settled that Udyam registration is prospective, never retrospective. Only services rendered on or after your registration date are covered. Everything Qravio billed before the certificate sits outside this entirely — you cannot go back and claim interest on old receivables.

What to do: put your Udyam Registration Number and the line "Registered Micro Enterprise under the MSMED Act, 2006 — payment due within 45 days" on the invoice template, in the MSA / order form, and in the vendor-onboarding pack. Buyers verify URNs live against the Udyam portal, so make yours easy to find. If someone stalls, MSME Samadhaan (samadhaan.msme.gov.in) is free, online, routes to the facilitation council in the buyer's state, is meant to conclude within 90 days, and produces a binding award. Cases are publicly visible, which tends to settle things before a hearing.

One caveat with a shelf life: §43B(h) and MSE procurement preference apply to micro and small only. A medium enterprise gets neither. This window is open for you now.

MSME Innovative Scheme

Saves ~₹19,000

Trademark "Qravio" at half fee — then claim the fee back

Government fee for a TM-A e-filing is ₹4,500 per class for an individual, a DPIIT-recognised startup or a small enterprise, against ₹9,000 for everyone else. The Udyam certificate is your proof of the concessional category, and it applies even if Qravio is a private limited company.

Hard rule

The certificate must be in hand at the moment Form TM-A is submitted. There is no later adjustment and no refund. You now qualify — filing before you had it would have cost double.

For a QR platform the classes that matter are 42 (software as a service — the core one), 9 (downloadable software, which covers your browser extensions), and arguably 35 if you want to claim the analytics and marketing side. Two classes at the concessional rate is ₹9,000 instead of ₹18,000.

Then go further: the MSME Innovative Scheme's IPR component (innovative.msme.gov.in) reimburses up to ₹10,000 of trademark registration cost, ₹15,000 for a design, ₹1,00,000 for a domestic patent and ₹5,00,000 for a foreign one. Net of the concession and the reimbursement, registering the Qravio wordmark can cost you close to nothing but the attorney's fee.

Governing policyPublic Procurement Policy
for MSEs, 2012

GeM seller terms

New revenue channel

Government becomes a reachable customer

Central ministries, departments and CPSEs are required to source at least 25% of annual procurement from micro and small enterprises (with 4% earmarked for SC/ST-owned and 3% for women-owned units). Registering on GeM as a Udyam-holding MSE gets you:

  • EMD exemption — no earnest money deposit blocked on any bid, typically 2–5% of bid value.
  • Tender fee exemption.
  • 15% purchase preference against non-MSE bidders.
  • Prior turnover and prior experience criteria waived on tenders up to ₹25 lakh.

That last one is the whole point for a company incorporated in February 2026. Turnover and track-record clauses are what normally lock a new vendor out of public procurement, and below ₹25 lakh they simply don't apply to you. In FY 2025–26, MSEs executed 68% of all GeM orders.

The procurable shapes of Qravio are real ones: asset-tagging QR for departments, tourism and heritage signage, municipal grievance QR, event and exhibition passes, menu and document landing pages. Dynamic QR with scan analytics is a line item a buyer can write.

Tier 2

Worth money when you need capital

Nothing to claim today, but the certificate is the precondition. Worth knowing before you walk into a bank.

SchemeCGTMSE

RBI priority sector
lending norms

Up to ₹10 cr, no collateral

Credit without pledging property

CGTMSE guarantee cover has been doubled to ₹10 crore. The trust absorbs 75–85% of the lender's loss, which is why a bank will lend against it without asking for property as security. The annual guarantee fee starts around 0.37% p.a. at small ticket sizes.

The services sector is explicitly eligible — IT and software qualify. The realistic ask for Qravio at this stage is a working capital limit or term loan somewhere in the ₹10–50 lakh range with no collateral behind it. Separately, lending to micro and small enterprises counts toward banks' priority-sector targets, so they have a quota reason to say yes that has nothing to do with liking your business.

Also announced and rolling out: the ME-Card, a customised credit card with a ₹5 lakh limit specifically for micro enterprises registered on Udyam, with a 30–45 day interest-free period. Useful as float against ad spend and cloud bills. Ask your bank whether they're issuing yet.

PlatformTReDS
(RXIL / M1xchange /
Invoicemart)

Turns receivables into cash

Discount a large client's invoice instead of waiting for it

Every company above ₹250 crore turnover must be onboarded to TReDS, and a June 2026 notification requires all CPSEs to settle MSME invoices through it. If Qravio lands an enterprise or PSU account, you can upload the invoice, have the buyer authenticate it, and let financiers bid to discount it — cash in days at competitive rates rather than a 45-day wait. Only available to you because you're a registered MSME.

Exhibiting abroad, part-funded

The IC Scheme reimburses stall charges and economy airfare for MSMEs participating in international exhibitions and trade fairs. Applications run in seasonal windows against a published calendar. Only relevant if Qravio decides to work a retail-tech, hospitality or packaging expo overseas — but if that's ever on the plan, this materially changes the cost of the booth.

State policyKarnataka Industrial
Policy 2025–30

Bengaluru Urban = Zone 3

Near zero for you

Karnataka's state layer gives a Bengaluru SaaS almost nothing

Worth saying plainly so you don't spend a week chasing it. Under the 2025–30 policy, Bengaluru Urban and Rural sit in Zone 3: no stamp duty exemption, the lowest capital subsidy band, and the incentives are keyed to eligible fixed capital investment — plant, machinery, land — which a software company doesn't have. The generous 25–40% investment promotion subsidies are for Zone 1 backward talukas.

The Karnataka money actually worth chasing is ELEVATE, the state's grant-in-aid programme: up to ₹50 lakh, fully non-dilutive, open at idea / product / revenue stage, with tracks for general, women-led and SC/ST founders. Note that it does not require Udyam or DPIIT — it's a separate track with its own periodic windows, so watch for the next call.

Income tax exemption

Udyam grants none. The tax holiday people mean is §80-IAC, which runs entirely through DPIIT recognition plus Inter-Ministerial Board approval — a different certificate on a different track. See below.

Adjacent

The other certificate you should hold

DPIIT Startup India recognition is complementary, not an alternative. It is free, takes one to three weeks, and the benefits barely overlap with Udyam's — but the case for it is not the one usually made.

Check this first

One fact decides whether any of this is available

DPIIT recognition is closed to sole proprietorships and Hindu Undivided Families. If Qravio trades as a proprietorship today, the question stops being "should I apply" and becomes "should I incorporate" — a materially bigger decision with its own compliance cost. Confirm the entity type before reading further.

The current framework is G.S.R. 108(E), notified 4 February 2026. It raised the turnover ceiling from ₹100 crore to ₹200 crore, kept the 10-year recognition window, and added a separate deep tech category with a 20-year window and a ₹300 crore ceiling. Older guides quoting ₹100 crore are out of date.

Applications are filed on NSWS (nsws.gov.in), not on the Startup India portal any more. There is no government fee. Turnaround is typically one to three weeks. The dominant cause of rejection is a vague innovation description — so lead with something concrete and specific to Qravio rather than a category label.

The actual reason to apply

Two funding rails you cannot reach any other way

  • SISFS — up to ₹20 lakh as a non-dilutive grant for proof of concept and prototyping, plus up to ₹50 lakh as debt or a convertible instrument for scaling. Requires DPIIT recognition and incorporation no more than two years before the date of application. Qravio was incorporated in February 2026, so that door closes around February 2028.
  • CGSS — the startup credit guarantee, open only to DPIIT-recognised entities. Cover per borrower was recently raised from ₹10 crore to ₹20 crore, with 85% of the amount in default covered up to ₹10 crore. This is a separate rail from the CGTMSE cover Udyam gives you; having both available matters for a company with nothing to pledge.
Why the timing argues for applying now

The last SISFS cycle closed 31 May 2026 and the next has no announced date. Applications route through an approved incubator, which takes preparation, and DPIIT recognition is a hard prerequisite that itself takes weeks. You cannot assemble this after a window opens. Recognition is free and has no ongoing compliance burden, so holding it unused costs nothing.

Karnataka's state startup incentives also generally gate on DPIIT recognition — but note they need a separate Karnataka Startup Cell registration on top, and ELEVATE specifically requires neither.

Reality checkIncome Tax Act, §80-IAC
Inter-Ministerial Board

SIPP — expired
31 March 2026

Do not plan around these

The two benefits people apply for, and why neither is the reason

§80-IAC is close to a lottery ticket. It exempts 100% of profits for any 3 consecutive years out of the first 10, but DPIIT recognition is only the prerequisite — the exemption itself needs separate Inter-Ministerial Board approval. As of April 2026, roughly 3,700 startups held an IMB certificate out of more than 1.97 lakh DPIIT-recognised entities, under 2%. It also only exempts profits, which Qravio does not yet have. The incorporation deadline runs to 31 March 2030, so get recognised now and approach the IMB later, when there is a real innovation case and something to shelter.

The IP benefits are mostly already yours or already gone. The 50% trademark rebate is the same ₹4,500 concessional rate your Udyam certificate gives you — no marginal gain. The 80% patent fee rebate is DPIIT-only but irrelevant unless you file a patent. And SIPP, which had the government pay your IP attorney's professional fees, expired on 31 March 2026 with no announced replacement, so new filings are privately funded end to end.

What does carry over with no overlap: self-certification under 9 labour and 3 environment laws for five years, and fast-track winding up under the IBC. Minor at 1–10 employees, but free.

Maintenance

How to not quietly lose it

The certificate never expires and needs no renewal — which is exactly why the annual obligation gets forgotten.

ObligationAnnual update against
ITR and GST filings

PAN + GSTIN linkage

Silent failure risk

Update it every year, right after you file your ITR

Udyam pulls investment and turnover automatically from your PAN/ITR and GST data, and registered enterprises are required to update and confirm those details annually. Let it lapse and the registration is suspended — at which point §43B(h) protection stops working, your GeM MSE exemptions stop applying, and scheme eligibility falls away. Nobody sends you a warning; you find out when a buyer's AP team checks your URN and it comes back inactive.

GSTIN is required for a permanent certificate, so keep the GST registration and the Udyam record consistent — the same legal name, the same PAN.
